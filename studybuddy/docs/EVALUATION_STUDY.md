# Evaluation study plan

## Research question

Does short, repeated use of StudyBuddy improve recall and confidence for selected computing learning objectives, and are the game instructions and feedback usable?

## Proposed participants and protocol

- Recruit 10–20 volunteer college students who study the selected topics; record recruitment and exclusions without storing identifying data in the report.
- Obtain informed consent and explain that participation is optional and does not affect grades.
- Select 2–3 learning objectives and prepare parallel pre-test and post-test forms with comparable item difficulty.
- Give a short pre-test, then ask participants to use the app for one week (or a documented supervised session for a classroom demonstration).
- Give the post-test and a five-point usability survey covering navigation, instruction clarity, feedback usefulness, motivation and perceived learning.
- Collect only pseudonymous participant IDs, test scores, completion/time data and survey responses. Store the participant key separately from results and delete it after analysis.

## Measures and analysis

Primary outcome: each participant's post-test percentage minus their pre-test percentage. Report sample size, median and range (and mean/standard deviation where appropriate), missing data, and the limitations of a small convenience sample. Usability: report item distributions and qualitative themes without identifying participants. Treat time, completion, accuracy and repeated mistakes as descriptive usage measures. Do not claim causation from an uncontrolled small sample.

## Status

This is a protocol only. No participants have been recruited, no scores have been collected, and no learning-improvement result is available. Do not fabricate results or present planned measurements as findings.
